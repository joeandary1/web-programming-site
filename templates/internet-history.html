Topic 1: History of the Internet

Introduction

The Internet is a global network that connects computers and devices around the world. It developed over many years through advances in networking, communication protocols, and computer technology.

Key Milestones

1969 — ARPANET

ARPANET connected several research computers in the United States.

Why it mattered?

It was one of the first major packet-switched networks and became an important foundation for the Internet.

Source: Internet Society

1983 — TCP/IP

ARPANET officially switched to TCP/IP.

Why it mattered?

TCP/IP allowed different networks to communicate using the same system.

Source: Internet Society

1983 — DNS

The Domain Name System was introduced.

Why it mattered?

DNS allowed people to use domain names instead of remembering numerical IP addresses.

Source: IETF

1986 — NSFNET

NSFNET connected universities and research centers.

Why it mattered?

It helped expand Internet access and became an important Internet backbone.

Source: National Science Foundation

1995 — Commercial Internet

NSFNET was shut down and commercial Internet providers became more important.

Why it mattered?

This helped the Internet expand to businesses and the general public.

Source: National Science Foundation

Three Key Internet Technologies

Packet Switching

Splits data into packets that can travel across a network.

TCP/IP

Allows different computers and networks to communicate.

DNS

Converts domain names into IP addresses.

Conclusion

The Internet and the World Wide Web are different.

The Internet is the network that connects computers and devices.
